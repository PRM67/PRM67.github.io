Words Matter

In his devotional, President Eyring shared how, as a young boy, he was shocked at one sacrament meeting when it seemed as though his father was enjoying a really boring talk. Young Hal Eyring asked him about it after the meeting was over, and his dad responded:

“Hal, let me tell you something. Since I was a very young man, I have taught myself to do something in a church meeting. When the speaker begins, I listen carefully and ask myself what he is trying to say. Then, once I think I know what he is trying to accomplish, I give myself a sermon on that subject.” He let that sink in for a moment as we walked along. Then, with that special self-deprecating chuckle of his, he said, “Hal, since then, I have never been to a bad meeting.”

I love this story, and I shared it when I gave a talk a couple years ago in one of my old wards. I shared it at the beginning of my talk, and then afterwards my roommate came up to me and was like, “Hey, do you want to hear the talk I gave myself, like in the story you shared at the beginning?” And at first I was like, oh, that's awesome. Because I really hope I don't give a boring talk.

All joking aside, though, I want to invite you to listen to the Spirit as I talk today, because the Spirit is the one that knows what you need to hear.

The topic that I have been pondering a lot recently is that our words really do matter. And the most important Word is our Savior, because one of His titles is the Word. Our Savior, Jesus Christ, is the perfect example of how we should communicate with others, and today I want to share how our communication should model that of our Savior's.

I recently had an experience where I was accused of saying something that I hadn't said, and it really impacted me. I strive each day to keep my communication positive, but I'm far from perfect. This experience helped to open my eyes a lot to how words are so powerful, because once said, they can never be taken back. Elder Charles A. Didier, in his talk “Language: A Divine Way of Communicating,” shared:

“One word could mean approval or denial, blessing or cursing, doubt or knowledge, friendship or enmity. Words can be articulated or mumbled, but every time a word is said, watch it, because it can never be retrieved. It is gone with the wind, gone forever.”

In the Bible, there are many accounts of how the Savior is particular with His words. One of the greatest examples is the story of the woman taken in adultery. The men that bring her to the Savior accuse and mock her. They talk about her as if she is an object to be discarded, a walking sin. Though the Savior could have condemned her right there in front of everyone, He chose His words very carefully. Rather than chew her out, He instead invited all the men around her to reflect on their own sins and mistakes, invoking in them the sense that they cannot blame her if they themselves are sinful as well.

As the prophet of God on the earth at that time, the Savior's words teach us that we should never use hateful language. We should never blame and point fingers. After the men leave, the Savior meets her where she is at and calls her “woman.” He stands up for her, and then He invites her to strive to be better.

When we are in a situation where someone is being laughed at, talked poorly about, mocked, or made fun of, are we someone that joins in because it is easy, or are we striving to follow the Savior's example and stand up for them, even if we are the only one?

President Nelson, in his talk “Peacemakers Needed,” shared:

“My dear brothers and sisters, how we treat each other really matters! How we speak to and about others at home, at church, at work, and online really matters. Today, I am asking us to interact with others in a higher, holier way. Please listen carefully. If there is anything virtuous, lovely, or of good report or praiseworthy that we can say about another person—whether to his face or behind her back—that should be our standard of communication.”

I know that our Heavenly Father and Savior Jesus Christ are only ever talking about their children in the holiest of ways. We sin and we make mistakes, yet each time we do, They invite us to come back to Them. They invite us into Their home. They love and support us. Our standard of communication should model Theirs.

I recently read a conference talk that has changed how I think about talking about others. The talk tells of a man named Elder Kirkham, who was blessed with a leadership opportunity in the Seventy. It talked about how in meetings he would rise to a point of personal privilege, and then, when recognized, he would proceed to say something good about someone else. Near the end of his life, he spoke briefly at Brigham Young University on the theme “Say the Good Word.” On the morning that Elder Kirkham died, Elder Hanks was invited to the Kirkham family home. There, he was handed a small, inexpensive notebook in which Elder Kirkham had kept his notes. The last two entries were “Say the good word” and “Your name is safe in our home.”

Ever since reading this talk, the phrase “Your name is safe in our home” has stuck with me. It has served as a reminder when I am talking about other people. What would it be like if we could all confidently tell anyone we interacted with that their name is safe in our home?

This is what it is like in the temple. As we walk into the house of the Lord, our names are used in the most sacred and reverent of voices. We are able to feel safe in the Lord's house, because we know that He will communicate with us in the best of ways. I know that the temple is such a special place on this earth, and that as we strive to make it the focus of our lives, we can be blessed with greater direction and a stronger resolve to follow the Savior.

As I have been thinking about all the ways to improve my communication, I recognize how much I take for granted my ability to talk with others and use my voice. I can say hi to people, I can call my mom, I can order food at a restaurant, and I can bear my testimony. Talking is something that I do every day of my life, yet I don't always realize how much of an impact it truly has.

What is more, I work with individuals with special needs almost every day, and I absolutely love it, but I work a lot with people that are nonverbal. They are unable to use their words to communicate, and therefore must communicate in other ways. I have learned so much through working with them, because rather than get frustrated with me, they are patient as they wait for me to try to understand what they want. As I put myself in their shoes, I recognize how important it is that we use our ability to talk for good, just as the Savior did.

Our Savior was never one to complain, and never spoke an unkind word. In Mosiah, one of my favorite phrases that talks about the Savior is when it says, “He opened not his mouth.” When I read this passage on my mission, I realized how the Savior went through so much, yet He never opened His mouth to complain. In Mosiah 14, which is quoting Isaiah, it says:

“He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb so he opened not his mouth.”

When I read this passage, I think of the amount of times I have opened my mouth purely to complain. I want to strive to be better, and rather than open my mouth to say something negative, I want to use my words for good.

Sister Bonnie H. Cordon, in her last general conference talk as Young Women General President, shared how important it is to use our words for good. She shared:

“In 2011, while I was serving with my husband as mission leaders in beautiful Curitiba, Brazil, my phone rang during a meeting. Rushing to silence it, I noticed the call was from my father. I quickly left the meeting to answer. ‘Hi, Dad.’ Unexpectedly, his voice filled with emotion. ‘Hi, Bonnie. I need to tell you something. I’ve been diagnosed with ALS.’ My mind swirled with confusion. Wait, what is ALS? Dad was already explaining: ‘My mind will stay alert while my body slowly shuts down.’ I felt my whole world shift as I began to wrestle with the implications of the grievous news. But on that unforgettable day, it was his last sentence that found a permanent place in my heart. My dear father said with urgency, ‘Bonnie, never give up an opportunity to testify of Christ.’”

This last statement from her father has always stuck with me, though I have room to improve when it comes to this invitation. I believe it is so important that we take greater advantage of the opportunities we have to testify of our Savior. I think I forget that it doesn't need to be a formal setting to share my love of the Savior verbally with those around me.

With this being said, I want to take this opportunity to use my words today to testify of the Savior Jesus Christ. I know that He lives. I know that He is the perfect example of how we should interact and communicate with others. He never said a negative word, and I know that as we follow His example, we can be blessed with His greater influence in our lives. And I say that in the name of Jesus Christ, amen.
