Interruptible

Sharing the hope that is within us through service

Paige Merkley

I am very grateful and very humbled to be here to speak with you all today. I have been praying for a long time now that as I speak I will be able to share the message that my Father would have you hear. I have learned a lot about a unique attribute of the Savior, and I'm excited to share with you what I've learned.

As I begin my talk, I would first like to share an experience I had a while back. I attended a Best Buddies talent show, and it was so much fun. Best Buddies is where you pair a volunteer up with an adult with special needs, and at the talent show there were songs, there were dances, there were Star Wars impressions, and they were all amazing because these friends with special needs just got up and did their best.

The talent that I will never forget, though, was around the middle of the show. The emcees got up and announced, “Next we will have so-and-so come up, and she will be snapping her fingers.” I was a little caught off guard, wondering what that would look like. But as I watched, this girl walked confidently up to the microphone. She held her hand right in front of the microphone, and she snapped her fingers just once. Then we were all silent, and she just stood back. We all stood up and gave her a standing ovation. We were all just so excited; it was so awesome. She stood up there and she snapped her fingers. She shared her talent with us, and we were so proud of her.

This experience has been so special to me for many reasons. I know that those same feelings of pride that we all felt for this girl, our Heavenly Father feels for us. He is proud of us not because we can do the same exact talent as everyone else, but because we can snap our fingers in our own way. We live in a time where comparison is so prevalent, and we can easily feel like we are failing if we are not accomplishing all that our peers are. But please know that this is not how our Heavenly Father meant it to be. Instead, what our Heavenly Father asks is that we give our absolute best each day, and that we help others as the Savior did along the way.

In their devotional address to young adults back in 2023, Elder and Sister Holland shared a message that I have come back to many times. Elder Holland started his message sharing:

“Sister Holland and I come tonight as the Apostle Peter said we should, ‘ready always to give … a reason of the hope that is in [us].’ We are going to talk about hope with the declaration that we must never lose it or its sister virtues, faith and charity. To do that, we must realize that hope is not just the message and manner of the naturally optimistic; it is the privilege of everyone who believes.”

The fact that hope is the privilege of everyone that believes is so interesting to think about, and I love that Elder Holland framed it this way—that when he and Sister Holland were interacting with us in the Church, that is what they were wanting to do: share the hope that was within them. As I have pondered this idea a lot, I feel very strongly that we have been blessed, whether we are called to serve as leaders or in a different capacity in the Church, with the opportunity to share the hope that is within us and the joy of the gospel of Jesus Christ.

One of the best ways to do this, in my opinion, is through service—service that is not compared to how others serve, but rather service that is in the ways of the Savior, and service that allows us to share our talents with others. Through my time at BYU, I have had the blessing of learning a lot about the importance of service. I have been blessed as I have found opportunities to serve, and also as I have felt the loving acts of others.

One of the most important lessons I have learned about ministering and service is that it is usually not convenient for the one serving, but it is in those moments that it is most important. Earlier this week I attended a speech about service, and something the speaker said really affected me. He said, “If you wait to have time to serve, you will never serve.” At first I was shocked at how strong this statement seemed, but the more I thought about it, the more I really believed it. We are constantly going through our lives with the next thing to check off, and if we are always saying, “When this is over, then I can help this person,” or “Then I can serve in this way,” chances are it doesn't usually get done.

A few years ago I was talking to a girl at institute, and she was sharing something that she had been working on lately that I have never forgotten. It has since become something that I try to work on daily. She shared with me that she had recently been praying to be interruptible. When first hearing this, I was confused about what that meant, but since then I have come to know what it means, and I have worked to do the same thing.

I pray to Heavenly Father in the morning to help me to be interruptible that day. I want Him to know that no matter what I have planned, or what I feel I need to get done that day, I am someone He can interrupt to help the person that needs to see His hand in their life. As I have done this, I have been amazed at what our Heavenly Father can do. So many times I have been rushing to the next thing when something comes up, and I have the opportunity to help in some way. The first thought always is, “I don't have time for this.” But then that thought is followed by, “Guess what? You prayed to be interruptible.” In those moments I laugh to myself, and I choose to make time to help.

I know that so many of us are overwhelmed by all that we feel we need to do. As individuals who have been called to serve in leadership positions at this time, we can feel like we have an extreme amount of things to get done, and I know that Heavenly Father knows this too. He will not ask us to do something that He will not prepare the way for us to accomplish. Instead, what He asks of us is that we be interruptible, and that we snap our fingers in the way that we do.

As I have learned about being interruptible, I have thought of so many times that the Savior was interruptible for people. One example that I love is in 3 Nephi chapter 1. It is right before the people are going to be persecuted and killed because the signs of the Savior's coming have not appeared. Nephi prays all day to God in behalf of his people, and he gets a response. In a podcast I listened to, they talked about this moment when the Savior speaks to Nephi. They talked about how it is possible that there was a lot of preparation the Savior was doing in this moment in heaven. He was about to come to earth to do His Father's will, and surely He was very busy. Yet He took the time to come to Nephi and say, “Lift up your head and be of good cheer.” He comforted Nephi and shared the hope that was within Him to say, “I will be there tomorrow.” The Savior shared this hope, and He was able to do it because He was ready to be interrupted.

As we have the opportunity to interact with others, we are truly able to make a difference in their lives as we work to share the hope and joy that we have found in the gospel of Jesus Christ. I have seen this so many times in my own life, and it truly has made a difference as other people have shared hope with me.

One example of this is my friend Kevin. I met Kevin at the Food and Care Coalition. I have the opportunity to go to the Food and Care Coalition each Friday night and eat dinner with people that are in tough spots in their lives right now because they are currently experiencing homelessness. One of the many friends that I have met is Kevin, and he is so much fun to be around. Every interaction I had with him was positive. I was always amazed at how he could show he cares about you, even in a short conversation with him. I don't remember everything that he told me, but I remember the happiness I felt after talking to him. This week I found out that Kevin passed away in an accident, and it has been really hard knowing that I never told him how much of a difference he made in my life, even through our few interactions.

I know that we can make a difference in other people's lives as we come ready to share the hope that is within us, and as we share our individual talents with them. President Hinckley once shared, “You never know how much good you do.” As we strive to be interruptible, please know that even if we feel like all we can do sometimes is snap our fingers, we can do so much good, even if we don't know it. We may never know the difference we make in someone's life, but we must always keep trying to do so. I know that our Savior will guide us to those we can help. I know that He sees us, and He sees them, and He wants to guide us to help His children. Amen.
