Good morning everyone! I am actually super excited to be here with all of
you! I feel like 1 know quite a few of you, and some of you I would say I know quite well, so 1 was really grateful when I found out I had been asked to speak in your ward! I have been trying to figure out what I wanted to share with you today for a while now, and not so ironically, as I speak I am going to be doing exactly what I will be talking about. The stake has invited us to speak on "Relying on the Savior" and I have learned a lot this week as I pondered this topic.
One of my favorite paintings ever is by Norman Rockwell. It is titled Lift up
thine eyes and I have it displayed right at my desk in my room. When looking at
it, your eyes are immediately drawn to the myriad of individuals that appear to be
hurrying around, their only focus being the next thing they need to do. They all walk with their heads turned down, not looking at each other, but all focused on getting to where they need to be. As you look up towards the top of the painting, there stands a reminder that I have needed on way too many occasions to count.
The painting setting is the sidewalk located right in front of a cathedral. Above the doors of the cathedral, a priest and his assistant are working together to put up a
sign, a sign that reads "Lift up thine eyes." The juxtaposition of this sign and all of the individuals that have no thought to look upward, is a very powerful reminder
to me. How many times do I focus on what I need to do and where I need to be,
and forget to look upwards and rely on my Savior to be the one to guide me?
This week I was reminded of a talk that really stood out to me when it was
shared. It is titled Call, Don't Fall. In it Elder Taylor G. Godoy recounts his
experience being in a hospital room and noticing signs around the room that said
"Call, Don't fall." After asking the nurse why the signs were everywhere she explained "It is to prevent a blow that might increase the pain you already have." As I was thinking about this phrase this week, I have realized how many times I tend to think I can do things on my own, and how many times I forget to simply
"call." And the thing about these "calls" is that our Heavenly Father and Savior Jesus Christ will always pick up. Elder Godoy shared: "In a world going through times of uncertainty, pain, disappointment, and heartbreak, we might feel inclined to rely more on personal abilities and preferences, as well as the knowledge and security that come from the world. This could cause us to put in the background the real source of succor and support that can counter the challenges of this mortal life."
So then, who do we call? Well, we have been invited to call, We get to call the greatest individual, the greatest friend, the greatest leader of all times. We get to call, we need to call, the Savior. The Savior is the perfect person to rely on, because He knows us perfectly. Something learned this week has helped me to
see this even more. In a podcast that follows the Youth church lessons each week, they were talking about the account of the woman at the well. They shared about how, when we think of our first encounter seeing the Savior, we imagine Him telling us He loves us, giving us a hug, and telling us all we have done good.
Yet this is not the experience of the woman at the well in the New Testament.
Instead, the first thing the Savior tells her is the worst sin she has committed. He
does not first tell her He loves her, instead, He points out how she needs to improve. Now hearing this sounds really harsh. No one wants to have their faults
be their first impression with the Savior. Yet in listening to this podcast, they talked of how the fact that the Savior pointed out the fault of this woman was indication that He really did know EVERYTHING about her. He didn't just say hey I love you, because many would think, well He probably wouldn't love me if He actually knew what I have done... Instead He loved this woman, and He loves us-and the fact that He knows every single one of our faults means that love is perfect and real. He doesn't just say it, He means it. He has seen us when we have fallen before, and He is there for us when we choose to call-but what I
have learned this week is that we have to choose to call.
In the Book of Mormon, I am always impressed by the account of Ammon and the Anti-Nephi-Lehites. The Anti-Nephi-Lehites had covenanted to not take
up weapons anymore, yet they were getting killed. Both Ammon and the King are worried, and both decide to turn to the Lord. First Ammon is the one that tells the King: " will go and inquire of the Lord, and if he say unto us, go down unto our brethren, will ye go?" The King responds saying, yes, even if we have to be slaves. Then Ammon is like: well don't worry about it, they have a rule that no one can be slaves. And then it is the King's turn to say, no no, let's inquire of the Lord, and then if He says to go, we will go. I think it is so cool how in this account, both men help each other to remember the importance of relying on the
Lord first. There are so many places in this day and age that we can turn to, but first and foremost, we have to rely on the Lord.
With this being said, one of the thoughts that has repeatedly come to mind
this week is that in order to rely on the Savior, we have to be willing to talk to the Savior. We have to call and be willing to counsel and we have to tell Him why we need to rely on Him. I fear I am really good at thinking I'm relying on the Savior, but really I am just keeping it all to myself rather than admit that I need help.
Fortunately, we have many opportunities and ways that we can remember to rely on the Savior. One of these is the sacrament. I love that when we partake of the Sacrament, we are able to reflect on how we did the week prior, and how we can
be better. I love that the Sacrament is every week, because every week I need the reminder to "Call and not fall." As we partake of the bread that represents the body of Christ, I always find it so cool that each piece of broken bread is different. because the Savior's Atoning sacrifice is individual and personal for each one of us. He does not want us to fall and cause greater injury, but instead He allows us to access the Atonement and feel of His great love and support. I personally am
so grateful for each Sunday when I am able to come and remember my Savior. I look forward to the Sabbath day, and I know that as we consecrate this day to be a day for the Savior, our reliance on Him will be fortified, our relationship with Him is strengthened and our question of "Who do I call?" can be quickly answered.
President Dallin H. Oaks in his first interview as president of the Church
was asked a question regarding what he thought young adults would need to
hear right now. His response is something hope to never forget. He simply said:
"Jesus Christ is the way." With so many possible responses, I love that He chose
the simple reminder that the Savior is the one we can rely on, the one we can
trust, the one we can depend on. In the Living Christ it says: "He is the light, the
life, and the hope of the world. His way is the path that leads to happiness in this life and eternal life in the world to come. God be thanked for the matchless gift
of His divine Son."
I know that the Savior is standing and ready to help us-but we have to be
willing to rely. He is there to help us through any trial, any struggle, anything
that we are going through. In this most recent General Conference, Elder Reid
said something that has really stood out to me. He said "You were never meant
to do this alone." Brothers and Sisters, today I want to testify that we are not
alone-and whatever mountain we are facing at this time, because we are all facing one, we don't have to do it alone. Please call and don't fall. Please know that we have a loving Savior who knows what you have done, knows what you will do, and is ready to be the light, life and hope of your world. I know that the Savior loves us, and He is there when we need to rely on Him. In the name of
Jesus Christ, Amen.
